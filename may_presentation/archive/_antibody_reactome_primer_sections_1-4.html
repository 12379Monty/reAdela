What is an Antibody?

The immune system’s job is to distinguish self from non-self — and to neutralize anything foreign. Antibodies are the precision instruments it uses to do that.

Y-shaped protein

Produced by B cells. Two binding sites at the tips of the Y.

Recognises an antigen

The molecular target the antibody binds.

Binds at an epitope

The specific 5–20 aa region of the antigen actually contacted.

~1015

unique antibodies the immune system can, in principle, produce

A note on peptide vs protein

A peptide is a short amino acid chain; a protein is a longer, folded one. The distinction is one of length and structural complexity, not kind. Antigens and antibodies are both proteins — what differs is their origin, function, and the immune logic that connects them.

§2.2 What is an Antigen?

Any molecule — almost always a protein or a fragment of one — that the immune system can recognize and respond to.

::: {.tag} Pathogens ::: ::: {.body} Viral coat proteins, bacterial surface proteins, toxins :::

::: {.tag} The host itself ::: ::: {.body} In autoimmunity, the immune system generates antibodies against its own proteins :::

::: {.tag} Environmental ::: ::: {.body} Allergens — pollen, food proteins, and other exogenous triggers :::

::: {.panel-eyebrow} Key insight ::: ::: {.panel-title} Antibody reactivity is fundamentally a peptide-level phenomenon — even when the antigen is a full-length protein.

The immune system responds to specific regions — epitopes — typically short stretches of 5–20 amino acids that happen to be physically accessible and structurally recognizable. :::

§2.3 What is an Antibody, Molecularly Speaking?

An antibody is a protein, encoded in DNA, produced by B cells. The remarkable thing is how its antigen-binding region comes into being.

V(D)J Recombination

Antibody genes are not fixed in the germline. Gene segments are randomly shuffled and joined within B cells.

…then Mutation & Selection

Rounds of somatic mutation refine binding affinity in response to antigen exposure across the organism’s lifetime.

Biographically informative

The antibody sequence is partly determined by the genome and partly by a stochastic, exposure-driven process — making the reactome biographically informative in a way the genome alone is not.

What is the Antibody Reactome?

Section 3

§3 What is the Antibody Reactome?

The complete set of antigen-binding reactivities present in a biological sample — typically blood serum or plasma — at a given point in time.

A snapshot of immunological history

  • Every pathogen encountered
  • Every vaccine received
  • Every self-protein mistakenly targeted
  • Every allergen that triggered a response

If the genome is the blueprint and the transcriptome is what’s being expressed, the reactome is a record of exposures and responses — written in the language of binding specificities.

For a data scientist

A high-dimensional, sparse count matrix

rows = individuals · columns = antigens
entries reflect whether (and at what level) an individual has antibodies that bind a given antigen

§4.2 Encodes Exposure History at Population Scale

At the population level, the reactome is a rich epidemiological signal.

::: {.num} 1 ::: ::: {.tag} Pathogen exposure ::: ::: {.body} Which pathogens has a community been exposed to? Reactivity patterns across individuals reveal community-level infection histories. :::

::: {.num} 2 ::: ::: {.tag} Vaccine effectiveness ::: ::: {.body} Which vaccines are working? Antibody signatures track real-world immune response, not just intended response. :::

::: {.num} 3 ::: ::: {.tag} Demographic clustering ::: ::: {.body} How do immune responses cluster across age, geography, and other demographic groups? :::

Patterns of reactivity across individuals → community-scale immunological insight.

§4.4 It is a Hard Measurement Problem

Until recently, measuring the antibody reactome comprehensively was not feasible. The breakthrough came with sequencing-based display methods.

::: {.eyebrow-small} From microarrays to sequencing :::

Protein microarrays

Thousands of antigens · expensive · limited content — the old way.

PhIP-Seq · MIPSA

Sequencing-based display: hundreds of thousands of antigens probed simultaneously across many samples; readout is a count matrix from NGS.
